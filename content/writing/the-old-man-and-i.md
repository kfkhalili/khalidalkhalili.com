The bench is a little wet from yesterday's rain. I can feel the moisture seeping through my pants and feel the sensation experienced when skin comes in contact with sodden wood.

The old man is sitting on the bench with me. We are throwing crumbs of bread to the pigeons loitering around. The birds, accept our kindness without question and move towards us in large waves until one of us makes an unusual move, the wave then begins to recede, their trust faltering.

The old man holds a rosary in one hand, his ragged fingers steadily counting the beads and sounding off a different name each time, methodically. The names reach the end, and the counting begins again.

"The world is a beautiful place.” He says to me as I stare at him, wide-eyed, completely oblivious to this "world" he mentions, with a consciousness bound to that of a normal eight-year-old child. I nod my head and smile.

"Was it like an adventure?" I ask him earnestly. "A never ending adventure, full of lost hopes, found love, and mended dreams". He raises his hand and puts it on my head and combs my hair with his fingers. He holds a small, fair hand in an overly large hand. My hand traces wrinkles in his, revealing calluses long ago discolored, and a scarred palm and thumb.

"Will you be going away again?" I ask him, beginning to feel sad. "Will you get me something with you when you come back?" rushing to replace sadness with the fleeting joy of possession.

"And what would a boy like you want?" The old man replies, smirking.

"A parakeet! Or a fez! Or perhaps a little drum?" The possibilities are endless and the mind of a child knows no bounds. The smile holds in the face of the wrinkled giant, "By the time we meet again; you will have no need for those things anymore."

I pull my hand out of his and fold my arms in the serious disapproval that only a child is capable of.

He laughs and hugs me.

He has long moved on now, and so have I, and the birds still continue their journey of trust, where we used to sit.
