“I think I finally know the reason.”

---

I say this to my driving companion but by then, he is too far to be reached by mere words and save for the shine of intelligence normally seen but not perceived in living creatures, one could scarcely say he was there at all. He sits there like a statue, impassive, his eyes locked on a road that stretches out infinitely in both directions. My line of thought trails off to observe his curious countenance and to find myself completely affected by his hidden determination to see our journey through.

I have heard a description of this meditative state that some experience and it relegates me to hesitation. They are either fortunate or unfortunate, depending on the observer's standpoint, to enter what has been called “The Box”, a singular state of focus or mindlessness that is the result of a failure or elevation of a mechanism in the human brain. In an unplanned and yet circumstantially attained moment, one would obtain a complete and converged awareness of everything and nothing.

A shared and perhaps defining characteristic in those heroes or victims of life and experience is that when their minds reach an overloaded state, and their bodies are bent or broken with incapacitating fatigue, they are transported to an isolated garden which depending on the season, may provide the fresh fruits of thought, memory, and imagination, and at other times, be overrun with the plain weeds of insipidity, stifled in the infertile soil of mindlessness, fenced in by the restrictive barrier of that undefined moment.

In a trance, the mind chooses a single object to focus on and continues to shrink the area of perception until nothing is thought of at all, the concentration being so complete that one could hardly remember how they got to where they were, let alone where they started from.

It could be a green-eyed fly, beating its wings, darting across the room on a warm autumn afternoon during a geography class. A simple outlet of pride it feels and wishes to display to those it believes are its ardent audience, the fly continues to accelerate around the room, feverishly expending its tiny life until it disappears, only to reappear after instilling doubt of whether it had been used up by its own energy. It could be a cigarette, kindled in a poorly lit room releasing its unrefined essence in a plume of smoke, hungrily consumed and changed forever by a person who, until moments ago, could not recall the tightness of his fingers around it in past experiences, and now ashes the cigarette with trained and unstrained precision. In the case of our driver, it might be the continuously revolving wheel of the car positioned in front of us, its fluid, unrestricted motion that swears of an unreal ability to move forever. It could be the continuous chiming of our speeding vehicle, a sharp sound capable of driving one to despair, but eventually dissolves into the subconscious as another unchangeable fact of life, or perhaps it could be the divine contemplations equal to that of the first men who discovered the magical properties of thunder and fire, tapping into a mystical source that provides a fountain of knowledge and subsequent wisdom.

That moment of lucid clarity could present itself in countless ways, serving as a passage for a vile and negative energy to disperse from a congested soul, or to open a living being to be used by a selfish force that sought a weakness of the mind and body to manifest itself, taking advantage in a moment of physical and mental sloth to devour the one thing we never had.

Time.

---

“Are you listening to me?” She calls me from the back seat.

“I… I'm sorry. Were you saying something?” Confused for a moment, I am recalled into the present.

“I've been talking to you! Where on earth did you go?”

“I'm... unsure.” I shrug.

---

Perhaps I was in The Box.
