The sun was beginning to set by the time they got to the deepest part of the forest, held completely still by an overwhelming silence, save for the sound of a stream running behind them. One would have expected the atmosphere to be dank and clammy with such surroundings, but it was dry with a warm, pleasant breeze, faintly scented with frankincense and cinnamon.

"That's funny. It should be around here somewhere." She said incredulously while holding out a map and pointing to a small section marked with a jet black pen.

Waiting for a few hours, they rested. Their journey had been long and full of perils, but they knew that if they found what they were looking for, it would have all been worth it.

"This is too dangerous, we shouldn't have come here." He thought solemnly while leaning on a rock; his right arm bent at the elbow behind his head.

Smiling for a moment she said, “Don’t worry. Your calculations are right. I know they are.”

“Perhaps it would have been wiser if we—”

“Be still. There, behind you.”

He turned around to see an old Oak across the stream, firmly positioned into the ground. Looking at the tree, his eyes immediately made out the creature standing on a naked branch, visible in the dark, a fiery hue hanging about it.

“You know, my calculations didn't account for the possibility of this animal being dangerous.” He whispered to her.

With the completion of his sentence, the bird took off towards them as if in response to his statement, flapping its wings violently as if expending energy from an infinite source. It swooped down in a flash and held a position of a few inches above the water as it glided across the stream, its feet leaving trailing marks and water vapor behind. The bird then began to rise and settled above a near treetop, into what could only be its nest.

“But of course! It was obvious that it would be here!”, she exclaimed delightfully in a whisper.

“Come, we need to get on top of this boulder for a better view” He quickly climbed up and extended a hand towards her.

As they settled on higher ground for better visibility, the creature unfolded its wings to show off an impossibly red plumage. They watched the spectacle in awe as the bird began to arrange twigs and pieces of bark around itself, paying fastidious attention to detail; following what seemed to be an absolutely sequential pattern only known to the bird itself. She pulled her sketchbook out of her backpack just in time to see the nest catch fire.

With furious intensity, she started to draw lines in every direction across the paper, taking her eyes off the bird only for moments at a time. He watched her as she drew as if held by a trance, feeling an amiable emotion known only to a person warmed by a bonfire on a bitterly cold night. He then heard the bird screeching as the flames consumed its body and turned to face it. Watching the animal writhe in torment, his pleasant feelings began to turn into a profound sense of sadness, sadness to see such a magnificent creature suffer that unspeakable agony.

He felt a hand on his shoulder and turned to find her looking at him, reading the unpronounced words in his face. “Don’t worry” she said, “It’ll be okay.” Her words hung in the air for what seemed to be an eternity as her warm smile continued to dissolve the morose feeling in the pit of his stomach.

He nodded and turned back towards the dying bird, consciously grasping the unspoken emotion, in an empathy only equivalent to sharing the experience itself.

They watched on in silence.
