In his mind's eye, what he sees fits together. Chaotic events, actions and words, but not organized chaos, it is what I might call harmonic chaos. Where conflicting concepts defy logic and concepts that came before them, and yet none could exist without the other. In absolute contrast and yet, they all have this completing quality. 'A skill a good film director has', I think to myself.

Conversation stretches for hours that feel like minutes, the discourse quickly closes a gap of months and his joy starts to return, tired of being away for so long, warily wondering if the moment it reaches, drops its bags on the floor and says "I'm finally home.", it would be asked to leave again.

'Do you think Syria will fall soon?', 'Where shall we travel next?', 'I'd like a strong cup of coffee, fancy some?', 'Do you still wear bright red lipstick?', 'So the Parisians were a bit stand-offish?', 'What do you think will come after Capitalism?'

The questions range from casual to personal, and it is personal when the answers begin to slip through the cracks between his fingers, like fine grains of sands that will stop for none. He fights the chill; I arrive and find him struggling and squirming; I throw down my bag pack to help him, I smile for home, but he's gone before we know what should have been done.

He kicks the covers over his exposed foot as the biting cold continues to dissolve the strangest chaos away.

He is awake.

Filled with a sudden urge to talk.

And it seems I must leave again.
