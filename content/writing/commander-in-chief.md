He soon found himself plucked out of a serene sleep and lashed to the helm, the wind frantically blowing in all directions. The raging sea was smashing at the frame, emitting sharp, shrill creaks and causing water to spray into his eyes with the tenacity several million droplets would have.

The moisture-laden air made it strenuous to breathe, and his heart pounded with effort as blood surged within his veins. He knew that he would not be able to keep at his arduous task for much longer.

“Roll up the sails!” He bellowed and yet could not hear his own words. He held on to the tiller as the deck slipped from under him.

“All is lost, Captain! We must abandon ship!” Cried one of the ship’s officers.

He turned to look at him. The officer was absolutely drenched from top to bottom as they all were, and one could glimpse water dripping from his beard, a quaint observation that the Captain would recall a few minutes later. It could be easily seen that he was terrified out of his wits, but this was not the time for theatrics. The Captain gritted his teeth and spoke in an icy-cold, guttural voice that would compel a grown man to quake in his boots.

“Like hell it is! We abandon ship when I say we abandon ship, now you assemble the men and roll up the sails or so help me God, I will have you dragged into a court of law and strung up by the thumbs, do you hear me Leftenant?!”

“Yes Captain!” The remonstration served to steel his nerves as he turned to follow instructions.

“And find me Maria, where the bloody hell is she?!”

Maria d’Ancone, one of the senior staff officers was an engineer by profession and a good one at that. He knew that she would be needed close at hand to establish a live assessment of the damage taken so far.

All was not lost. It could not be. He would make sure of it.

Soon after, Maria could be seen hurrying towards him, her chestnut hair blowing to her side throughout that it seemed to be held in place, the sight was stranger still as she carried herself with the calm that confirmed the presence of a calculating mind. Her confidence served to bolster his own, but as she got closer he could discern a troubled look on her face. This worried him.

“Captain, we have bigger problems than this storm.” She shouted hurriedly.

“Like hell we do! What the devil is it now?”

She began to speak but a sudden ringing noise drowned out her voice and all he could see was the movement of her lips. The storm ceased and the rocking stopped, the scene blurred and then faded away into nothing.

I opened my eyes. My hand reached out for the alarm and silenced it for the next twenty-four hours.

Good morning world.
