I started writing this in Dubai. I wrote it on a piece of paper and kept it in my wallet for around a month before travelling to India and feeling the necessary awareness to complete it.

---

The sound of the concrete jungle beckons me.

A whirlpool of avarice and desire, ant hills rise by the thousands in this city. A question poses itself in my head: "What have we traded for these structures we call our homes?" The inquiry is repeatedly asked, and yet no answer reveals itself.

The query transforms and delves deeper into other aspects of life around me. We industrialize the creation of food to nourish an ever-growing population and invent medication to stave off thousands of diseases that came into being yesterday. The cycle unfolds as we attempt to nullify damage created by a life set to satisfy our ravenous desires. We force feed our cows so they'll produce milk that is ample in quantity but is left wanting in the reasons we originally chose to consume it, we acknowledge such deficiencies and pump nerve-damaging supplements into our veins to compensate this loss. My mind is staggered by the number of scenarios that follow this exact same format. Air conditioning that regulates our air but creates chemicals to pollute the environment. Cars that weaken our legs and reduce them to that of the frail and the infirm and processed water to yield our hair to the drain.

The list is endless.

Transported back to the forty-something floor of a skyscraper, I am returned to this metropolitan city. This one, like others, was designed to house the largest possible number of us, stacking millions of us closer together and yet effectively, rendering us more alone. "What is the price?" and perhaps more importantly, "What are we buying?”

I am neither a defeatist, nor do I look down upon human achievement. Our ability to adapt through conceived solutions produces a joy in me that knows no bounds. This is not a proposition to dismantle our metropoleis. This is food for thought, a quiet voice in your head that whispers softly into your ear, so that one day it may convince you to change the mechanism that drives us, to perhaps cater more closely to what our hearts truly desire.

---

Those were my thoughts then, now as I sit on the balcony of a rented room in Himachal Pradesh, gazing at a captivating sight of trees and mountains, I feel a gentle breeze on my face, and my mind enters a more lucid state.

We have bought technology. We have bought ventilation, clean water, heating, reliable electricity, functional bathrooms. We have bought rooms that don't smell, clothes with no stains, and we have bought polished floors.

The above should provide some insight into how I've been living while on this trip, it is full of minor inconveniences and small hardships. However, even with the above, I still ask: "What are we buying?"

Perhaps this is a ruse to squeeze the most out of a deal, keeping in character with the city dweller that I am or perhaps the trickery lies in the deal itself.

A moment overtakes me and stretches out into the folds of time.

"What... What have we lost?"

We have lost the right to a nutrient-rich meal that a plowman's body demands, not desires. We have lost the ability to turn a skill into revenue without approvals from people who produce nothing. We have lost the freedom to roam the world unmolested on this earth that belongs to all of us. We have lost the freedom to build a temple in the middle of the woods to worship Gods proclaimed by none on grounds that belong to no one. We have lost the closeness the family of five feels on a moving motorcycle. A father riding with his young daughter in front of him, his wife sitting behind him, cradling their infant son in her arms. Their son, by the looks of it he could be eleven or twelve, sits with his arms wrapped around his mother, securing her in place. We have lost the passion of direct consequence that tells us that our own survival is an unrelinquishable responsibility and that the world does not owe us free health care or education. We have lost the fruits of our sweat and blood to a government we cannot relate to, to provide prestige for a country we cannot recognize. We have lost loyalty to that which was our home, and replaced it with the blind following of leaders we do not approve of to fight in wars that close the conscience in our hearts from a rifle scope aimed at a child we never hated.

---

I've returned from India since, and as I transcribe the above text from my journal onto a computer past midnight, I rush my work to sleep and wake up to a world that I have never liked but have never had the courage to cast away. And so I ask you once more before I go:

What did we buy? And what was the price?
