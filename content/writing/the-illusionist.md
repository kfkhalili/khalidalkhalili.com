He shuffles the cards violently. A look is all it takes to make him know it is working. He is aware that their eyes watch in vain. The produced happiness is fleeting, but it creates an addiction for more. Once a man, he is momentarily transformed into a demigod, an assumed fountain of the innermost secrets of the universe. A slight of hand and the blink of an eye is all it takes to make him know it is working.

Once the crowd is captured, the performer gives birth to an illusion that is sworn to be real. A momentary lapse of faith, the force of shock causes the audience to question their beliefs, to doubt all the things they had been taught as children.

He basks in an imaginary light as his confidence builds. The fingers that were once unsure, now move in fluid, undisturbed motions. They produce a memory held in the innermost fibres of his muscles and as his performance reaches its peak, he pulls the carpet from under his innocent spectators and they are thrown into an awe that is most genuine. His joy rises further.

The more he performs, the more he is amazed by the nature of the human mind and the imperfections in it. And it is that secret knowledge that allows him to further weave a reality suspended from their own.
