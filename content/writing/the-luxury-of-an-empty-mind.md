It is with a curious reflectiveness that I write these words.

Dear reader, mark me well, for some things truly are stranger than fiction. It is but this evening, in the company of a few friends that my mind began to wander unto a peculiar and meandering path onto which I was taken back to a dream I once had several years ago.

The odd thing about such a reminiscence is the detailed recollection of an unbidden thought. It had come to me uncalled, confusing me further still that I had previously failed to evoke it and hence thought nothing of it until then.

The dream, or shall I say intuition, for it seemed to be of a foretelling nature, presented itself to me in a fever-induced sleep on a warm autumn evening that had me shaking throughout most of that night. I recall ever so vividly standing on the brink of a calm and desolate ocean. A complete vacuum, save for the soft sound of the waves touching the shore softly and retreating, the surf leaving its damp imprint on the sand only to have it erased once more, two lovers forever whispering a sincere promise that neither of them would ever be able to keep.

As I stood on that threshold, reality began to dissolve around me, I would like you to imagine the experience to be akin to leaving your memories behind, connected to a road you have never traveled on, unwinding backwards to a place you never came from, where fruits grew on trees you never climbed, in an orchard you had never seen. And yet, you knew that those memories belonged to you, did at some point, or perhaps would someday. Your perplexity is understandable, dear reader, I assure you that I had similar sentiments during what was otherwise, a moment of complete clarity.

I found myself inextricably in an attic of an ancient house with three men, decades older than I and in an instant I knew, inexplicably, enigmatically, that they were brothers from a multiple birth. They stood huddled together, leaning on each other with their backs crooked and wordlessly pointing to a dusty chest in the center of the cramped space.

The muddled senses and the nature of disbelief in our dreams render us acquiescent and amenable, urging us into an all too eager and compliant state. I moved towards the chest, dragging my compelled feet to arrive at an awaiting destiny, the hours this process lasted were of an excruciating form, it was not of fear as you might have imagined, oh no dear reader, but of an inquisitiveness to see this arduous task through. When I arrived at my destination, the old man standing in the center produced an iron device from his robes and presented it to me in the center of his thin-skinned palm. Ever so cautiously, I extended my arm to his wrinkled and craggy appendage and retrieved what, on closer inspection, turned out to be an antique-looking key.

Fitting the lock snugly, I turned the key with a resounding clank, and I felt, at that time, that the chest would forever remain unlocked.

Ever so slowly, I opened the chest and within it felt my mind disintegrate.

The sudden roaring laughter had startled me, and I found that I had missed the punch-line. I smiled cordially while trying to ascertain what had elapsed, carefully inspecting my mind’s integrity by sifting through inert memories, bringing life to them once more.

Overpowered by a sense of melancholy, I found myself putting my thoughts in a dark recess to await inspection, perhaps someday soon, at a more suitable time.

And now before I lay my head to sleep, I can discern a voice, telling me that I will be dreaming again tonight.
